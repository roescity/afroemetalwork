Fireplace

Mild steel  ·  62″ × 94″ × 16″  ·  2009

In a private collection.

More views

This large fireplace, mantle and surround is a free-spirited interpretation of fire and wood. The upright support forms are an obvious reference to flames as they twist and turn back on themselves and seek to consume all that is above. Both flanking flame forms are welded to a “bead-board” that provides an architectural columnar reference and is a linear counter point to the organic flame shapes. Spanning the full width of the fireplace above the actual firebox, rondels of end cut logs appear to support the mantle. Made of mild steel painted and then waxed the overall appearance is of bronze. The firebox for storing wood, similarly finished provides the practical support of “feeding the fire”.
