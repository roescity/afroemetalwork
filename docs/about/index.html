Artist Statement

I work in welded steel and bronze, cutting, bending, hammering, and combining hard material into forms that feel fluid, organic, and alive. My sculptures often begin with a seminal idea that might be portrayed as a visually realistic form. I prefer, however, to play with the elemental shapes and how they combine to allow the viewer to imagine a different reality or potential. The sharp edges of my forms create a line that moves the eye within and around the overall piece and ideally will compel the viewer to explore the interplay of elements from different vantage points. I want the aesthetics of my work to be pleasing, and to leave enough room for interpretation such that each viewer will respond uniquely to a piece. Ideally, what one sees can change with each visit, so that the work remains fresh over time. I love that I can express myself artistically but also solve practical problems, often with an unexpected aesthetic inclusion.

About

Alisa Formway Roe is a Pacific Northwest sculptor working in mild steel, corten or “weathering” steel, stainless-steel, and bronze. She earned a B.A. in Art History from the University of Washington in 1979. In 2005, after many years raising a family, she returned to making art through sculpture and welding classes at Portland Community College and later established her own studio. Her work ranges from intimate bronze figures to large outdoor sculpture and functional metalwork. It has been exhibited in Oregon, Washington, and Illinois and is held in the public collections of the City of Lake Oswego and Portland Community College, as well as in private collections.
